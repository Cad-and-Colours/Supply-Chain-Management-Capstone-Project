## Overview

### What if a 2.4% tax on revenue could wipe out more than 20% of a company's profit?

This capstone examines the supply chain of **Medical Technologies Corporation (MTC)**, a medical device manufacturer facing growing costs, rising inventory, logistics inefficiencies, and a revenue-based Medical Device Excise Tax.

The challenge was not simply to cut costs — **it was to redesign the supply chain so that MTC could absorb the tax while maintaining service quality, customer relationships, and operational flexibility.**

Using concepts from **Supply Chain Logistics, Operations, Planning, and Sourcing**, I analyzed MTC's existing supply chain and developed a set of strategic recommendations covering demand forecasting, logistics, distribution, inventory, and supplier management.

## The Challenge

MTC's revenue was growing, but its profitability was under pressure. The existing supply chain involved multiple logistics nodes, outsourced sterilization, fragmented inventory, and significant sales-related costs.

The analysis focused on three key questions:

- **Where are the major sources of cost and inefficiency?**
- **How can demand and inventory be planned more effectively?**
- **How can the supply chain be redesigned to reduce cost without compromising customer service?**

## Key Analysis

- **Demand Planning & Forecasting** – Compared traditional flat forecasting with seasonal forecasting and developed a 2015 demand forecast.
- **Logistics & Distribution** – Mapped the existing supply chain and evaluated transportation delays, distribution nodes, and inventory positioning.
- **Inventory Optimization** – Examined the relationship between demand patterns, production, shipments, and inventory levels.
- **Operations & Cost Optimization** – Identified opportunities to reduce logistics, handling, inventory, and sales-related costs.
- **Sourcing Strategy** – Developed a strategic supplier model based on shared forecasting and joint inventory management.
- **Supply Chain Redesign** – Developed an improved logistics model combining in-house sterilization, an owned 3PL network, and dual-channel distribution.

## Strategic Recommendations

### 1. In-house Sterilization
Bring sterilization into the manufacturing facility to eliminate approximately **48 hours of transportation-related lead time**.

### 2. Owned 3PL Network
Reduce dependence on traditional distributors by developing an owned logistics network with greater control, visibility, and responsiveness.

### 3. Dual-Channel Distribution
Use direct distribution for approximately **80% of routine surgeries**, while retaining sales representatives for complex and emergency cases where their technical support adds value.

### 4. Strategic Consolidation
Pursue partnerships or mergers with complementary manufacturers to build scale, expand product offerings, and strengthen negotiating leverage.

### 5. Strategic Supplier Partnerships
Move from a transactional supplier relationship toward shared forecasting, joint inventory management, and long-term cost reduction.

## Key Result

The forecasting analysis showed a **98.84% reduction in Mean Squared Error (MSE)** when seasonal forecasting was compared with traditional flat forecasting.

The overall strategy aims to create a **leaner, faster, and more responsive supply chain** while offsetting the financial pressure created by the revenue-based excise tax.
